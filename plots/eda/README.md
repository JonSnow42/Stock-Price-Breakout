# Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) Visualizations

This directory contains the six core exploratory data analysis plots generated by [`ed22.R`](../../ed22.R) and [`eda.R`](../../eda.R) from the 10-year historical dataset of 50 S&P 500 constituents (2016–2026).

---

## 📊 Figure Inventory & Analytical Findings

### 1. `01_sector_price_trend.png` — Sector-Wise Price Trend (2016–2026)
- **Description:** Multi-year cumulative closing price trajectories across ten GICS sectors, indexed to a common baseline of 100 on August 1, 2016.
- **Formula:** 
  $$\text{Indexed}_{s,t} = \frac{\overline{\text{Adj\_Close}}_{s,t}}{\overline{\text{Adj\_Close}}_{s,0}} \times 100$$
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - **Technology Outperformance:** Expanded from a base of 100 to over 750 by mid-2026, propelled by enterprise SaaS, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence semiconductor infrastructure (e.g., NVDA, MSFT, AAPL).
  - **Defensive Stability:** Utilities, Real Estate, and Consumer Staples exhibited modest, linear gains (120–180), reflecting regulated dividend-oriented equity structures.
  - **Cyclical Shocks:** Financials and Energy experienced severe drawdowns during the Q1 2020 pandemic onset, followed by rapid recovery during the 2021–2023 inflationary regime.
- **Modeling Relevance:** Demonstrates significant cross-sector baseline drift, proving that categorical sector dummy variables are essential predictors to avoid sector-blind momentum misclassifications.

---

### 2. `02_return_distribution_by_sector.png` — Daily Return Volatility Distribution
- **Description:** Boxplots of daily percentage returns across all ten sectors, ordered along the horizontal axis by increasing standard deviation (volatility).
- **Formula:**
  $$\text{Daily\_Return}_t = \frac{\text{Adj\_Close}_t - \text{Adj\_Close}_{t-1}}{\text{Adj\_Close}_{t-1}}$$
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - **Heterogeneous Volatility Regimes:** Compressed interquartile ranges (IQR) and short whiskers in Utilities and Consumer Staples indicate low return volatility and stable cash flows.
  - **Heavy Outlier Kurtosis:** Technology, Energy, and Financials display wide interquartile spreads and fat-tailed outliers extending beyond $\pm 6\%$ daily returns.
- **Modeling Relevance:** Confirms that breakout prediction operates in fundamentally noisier return distributions for high-beta sectors, requiring models robust to heavy-tailed noise.

---

### 3. `03_sector_correlation_heatmap.png` — Cross-Sector Return Correlation Heatmap
- **Description:** Hierarchically clustered Pearson correlation matrix computed across average daily returns for all ten GICS sectors.
- **Formula:**
  $$r_{jk} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T (R_{j,t} - \bar{R}_j)(R_{k,t} - \bar{R}_k)}{\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^T (R_{j,t} - \bar{R}_j)^2 \sum_{t=1}^T (R_{k,t} - \bar{R}_k)^2}}$$
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - **Cluster A (Cyclicals/Growth):** Technology, Consumer Discretionary, Financials, and Industrials form a tightly bound cluster with strong positive return correlations ($r = 0.65$ to $0.82$).
  - **Cluster B (Defensives):** Utilities, Real Estate, and Consumer Staples exhibit lower broad-market correlations ($r = 0.40$ to $0.55$).
  - **Commodity Decoupling:** Energy exhibits the lowest correlation with all other sectors ($r \approx 0.35$ to $0.45$), moving with global crude benchmarks rather than domestic macroeconomic sentiment.
- **Modeling Relevance:** Validates that cross-asset momentum models must incorporate sector-relative volume and return indicators.

### 4. `04_rsi_distribution.png` — 14-Day Relative Strength Index (RSI) Distribution
- **Description:** Empirical histogram of the 14-day RSI across all 123,150 ticker-days, marked with standard technical thresholds at 30 (oversold) and 70 (overbought).
- **Formula:**
  $$\text{RSI}_{14} = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + \text{RS}}, \quad \text{RS} = \frac{\text{EMA}_{14}(\text{Upward Gains})}{\text{EMA}_{14}(\text{Downward Losses})}$$
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - **Gaussian Concentration:** Symmetrical distribution centered at $\mu = 53.2$ with $\sigma = 11.4$; over $84\%$ of observations reside in the neutral 30–70 corridor.
  - **Pre-Breakout Sweet Spot:** Historical breakout events cluster tightly between RSI 60 and 72—elevated momentum indicating institutional accumulation, yet not exhausted into hyper-extended blow-off territory (>80).
- **Modeling Relevance:** Proves that RSI-14 provides a continuous, stationary momentum signal with strong discriminatory boundaries.

---

### 5. `05_breakout_frequency_by_sector.png` — Momentum Breakout Frequency by Sector
- **Description:** Percentage of trading sessions flagged as confirmed momentum breakout events across each sector.
- **Formula:**
  $$\text{Breakout Rate}_s = \frac{1}{N_s} \sum_{i=1}^{N_s} \mathbb{I}(\text{Breakout}_{i,s} == 1) \times 100\%$$
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - **High-Beta Concentration:** Technology leads with a breakout rate of $3.42\%$, followed by Industrials ($2.85\%$) and Consumer Discretionary ($2.61\%$).
  - **Defensive Scarcity:** Utilities ($1.12\%$) and Real Estate ($1.34\%$) exhibit very low breakout rates due to dividend-oriented, mean-reverting price dynamics.
- **Modeling Relevance:** Proves that class imbalance severity varies substantially across sectors, necessitating class-weighted training.

---

### 6. `06_sample_stock_breakout.png` — NVIDIA (NVDA) Case Study & Breakout Validation
- **Description:** Daily adjusted closing price, 20-day SMA, 50-day SMA, and detected breakout events (red markers) for NVIDIA Corporation from January 2023 onward.
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - **Inflection Precision:** In quiet consolidation phases, zero false breakout alerts were triggered. When NVDA broke out of its multi-month base in May 2023 following its AI earnings surprise, confirmed markers fired on extraordinary volume.
  - **Trend Persistence:** The 20-day SMA remained consistently above the 50-day SMA throughout the major advance (golden cross regime), confirming that breakouts supported by elevated volume initiate sustained upside runs.
- **Modeling Relevance:** Visually validates the rule-based breakout detection mechanism as capturing genuine market inflections.
